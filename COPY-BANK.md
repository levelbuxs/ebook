## How to open (the single most important decision)

**Rule: open with your three biggest, most specific items.** "$499 e-bike" beats "expensive
items" every time — the brain can picture an e-bike; it can't picture "savings."

> Last month a **$[499] e-bike**, a **queen mattress**, and a **$[499] wine fridge** showed up
> at my house. I paid **$0.00** for all three.

Alternatives:

- **Secrecy tease:** "I'm not naming the store, and I'll tell you exactly why in a second.
  It's the opposite of what you're thinking."
- **Anti-coupon:** "Coupons are a 20% off hobby. This is a 100% off hobby."
- **Confession:** "I need to say upfront this sounds like a scam. It isn't, and I'm not going to
  argue about it — just watch the video where I do it without cutting the screen."

## Section starters

For each section: the **job** it has to do, then three openers.

### 1. Hero / above the fold
*Job: create disbelief + curiosity in under 3 seconds. Do not explain the method yet.*

- "Last month a $[499] [item], a [item], and a $[499] [item] showed up at my house. I paid
  $0.00 for all three. I'm not naming the store, and I'll tell you exactly why in a second."
- "There's one major retailer running a private reviewer program. Almost nobody gets past
  the application — most people apply once, get rejected, and assume they're not eligible. I got in."
- "I'm not saying 'free stuff' like a scammer — I'm showing you a receipt that says $0.00."

### 2. The big-ticket wall
*Job: make them think "no way that was free."*

- "Not samples. Not travel-size. Full-size, full-price items that were in my house within a
  couple weeks of being approved."
- "Every one of these has a video. And my @handle in frame, so you can check it's really me."
- "Here's the part people don't believe: the wine fridge, the e-bike, the queen mattress.
  All for zero."

### 3. How it works
*Job: reframe the failure as mechanical, not personal.*

- "It's not luck. It's an application problem. Most people try once, get rejected, and decide
  they're not eligible. They are — they just didn't set the account up right."
- "There's an order to this. Four things, in sequence. Do them out of order and nothing
  happens — no error, no email, just silence."
- "The program is real and it's not hidden — what's hidden is how the application is actually
  scored."

### 4. Photo proof
*Job: shut down "pics or it didn't happen" before they think it.*

- "Screenshots, not stories. These are all off my own phone, with my handle in frame so you
  can check it's actually me."
- "I'm blurring my address because I'm not an idiot. Everything else is exactly as it came
  off my screen."
- "Photos can be faked, so here's the uncut screen recording — including the boring part
  where I'm waiting for the page to load."

### 5. Video proof
*Job: remove the last doubt.*

- "Watch me do it, start to finish, no cuts. I say the date out loud, I show the prices, I
  land on the confirmation. The boring parts are still in there — that's the point."
- "I shot this on my phone in my house. The handle in the corner is mine. The list is real.
  The prices are real. The $0.00 is real."
- "If I were going to fake this, I'd have cut the waiting part. I didn't."

### 6. What's inside
*Job: make the guide feel like the key to something real.*

- "The store, the program, the application link, and the exact setup sequence. Short on
  purpose — you should be able to apply tonight."
- "Chapter 2 covers new account vs. 12-month-old account — including which one is actually
  faster. It's not the one you'd guess."
- "No fluff chapter about mindset. Chapter 1 gives you the store. Chapter 4 gives you the
  link. Chapter 5 tells you what to do if you hear nothing."

### 7. Price
*Job: make $29.99 feel small next to the value, without fake urgency.*

- "$29.99. That's less than the last thing you bought on Amazon and forgot about. One
  mattress covers this 13 times over."
- "I priced it at $29.99 because that's below the point where people need to think about it.
  I'd rather you just try it."
- "It's $29.99 today. It goes to $[49] when I finish the [update] section — the price on
  the button is the real price." ← only if true

### 8. Close
*Job: one last small push. Never beg.*

- "You'll spend more than $29.99 on stuff you don't even want this month. An hour from now
  you could have the application submitted. Or you could keep scrolling."
- "Worst case you're out $29.99 and you know the trick. Best case a $[499] [thing] shows up
  next week."
- "If it doesn't work you email me, I send your money back, and you keep the guide."

## Rules

1. **Specific beats impressive.** "A $499 e-bike" beats "expensive electronics."
2. **Name the objection first.** If you don't say "this sounds like a scam," they say it
   to themselves anyway.
3. **Never cut the video during the claim.** The boring parts are what make it believable.
4. **The "here's what it isn't" paragraph is the highest-leverage paragraph in the ad.**
   Never cut it.
5. **One fake screenshot ends it.** Don't ship a claim you can't back up.
6. **Fake urgency costs more than it makes.** No countdown timers unless the deadline is real.
7. **Keep the "skip this if" column.** Turning away bad-fit buyers drops refunds and
   chargebacks to near zero.
8. **Protect the mechanism.** Never say "Walmart reviewer program" in public. Use the
   secrecy as the hook, not the retailer as the reveal.
